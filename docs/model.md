# Continuous-time mixed-integer model

Customer `sequence` is immutable routing order. `ready_at` is an input specifying when that leg becomes available, not an optimiser-selected departure; `accepts_at` is earliest service start. `deadline` applies to arrival. `arrived_at` and `completed_at` are execution facts. If a supplied final delivery is explicitly marked as a depot return, it occupies the terminal position without adding a second return. The supplied return must actually identify the depot location.

For gap i and candidate station/port c, binary y(i,c) selects the visit. At most one y is selected per gap. Continuous variables represent departure, charging start/end, arrival, service start/end, battery arrival/exit and charge energy. Each candidate's delivered energy is a continuous difference, not one of a list of target SoCs. A minimum positive charge of 0.001 kWh distinguishes an actual visit from an inactive variable; this is an explicit model bound.

Leg start is derived exactly as `max(previous activity end, ready_at)`; for the first leg, previous activity end is `max(current simulation time, active service completion)`. There is no review-time allowance. An equality/max constraint prevents the solver delaying departure to improve cost. It can still choose a later charging slot, in which case the vehicle travels when ready and waits at the charger. The executor applies the same readiness rule, retains deterministic durations and never shifts a committed charging slot. `depart` is kept for timing calculations and persisted execution progress, not as a separately scheduled decision.

Battery balance subtracts direct travel consumption, then adds selected charge energy minus the detour's extra consumption. The same equation covers no-charge gaps. Energy lies within effective capacity (nameplate × SoH); every arrival and final return respects the selected reserve. A vehicle already physically at a compatible charger can plug in below reserve, including zero energy, without a fictitious driving leg. A below-reserve driving exception requires recovery and explicit manager acknowledgement. Energy cannot become negative. Health faults or temperature at/above 60 °C prohibit movement until conditions change or assistance is supplied.

Times include travel with a configurable allowance, customer service, readiness/acceptance waiting, connection and release. Every charge occupies its port from its reserved start through release. A disjunction places it wholly before or after each confirmed booking. Another set excludes intervals where its full reserved power would exceed the site's capacity. Empty interchangeable ports are represented once until their booking calendars differ. This is symmetry reduction, not a loss of feasible charging capacity.

Charging duration is piecewise linear in battery energy. For a band [L,U], delivered energy is clamp(Eout−L,0,U−L) − clamp(Ein−L,0,U−L). Binary segment selection represents each clamp exactly. Band minutes are delivered energy × 60 / (power × efficiency × taper multiplier). The taper bands match the independent physics implementation.

Let F(t) be the integral of the applicable piecewise-constant tariff from the horizon origin to t. F is represented as a piecewise-linear function. A constant-power band's billed cost is power × taper × [F(end)−F(start)] / 60. Summing bands gives exact tariff-crossing cost. Extra valid inequalities bound each band's cost below by its purchased energy times the minimum tariff, improving the MILP relaxation. The validator independently advances the shared charging physics through tariff boundaries and recomputes metered energy, duration and cost.

Tariffs use half-open local IST minute intervals, including midnight wrapping. An equal start/end means all day. Station intervals override matching depot intervals; otherwise the station base price applies. Highest priority wins; stable tariff ID breaks ties. Validity dates are inclusive. Connection/release do not draw charging energy. Costs remain unrounded internally; rounding happens only in the display.

Normal lexicographic objectives are (1) cost, (2) final completion. Recovery objectives are (1) count of late customers, (2) maximum lateness, (3) summed lateness, (4) cost, (5) completion. Each proven phase fixes its best value within numerical tolerance before the next phase. A time-limited cost incumbent can receive a remaining-budget completion refinement with visits fixed; that result remains `FEASIBLE`, never globally optimal. The separate fastest alternative reverses the final cost/completion priorities and is labelled accordingly.

Model tolerances: 0.0002 kWh energy validation, ₹0.01 session/total cost validation, 0.1–0.15 second reconstructed travel/charge timing tolerance; PostgreSQL operational JSON preserves ISO timestamps; MongoDB history dates use millisecond precision. Strict ordering/conflict comparisons allow 0.05 second numerical noise. Lexicographic objective tolerance is 0.00001 in the current objective's units; HiGHS relative MIP gap is 1e-7. Safety limits must be configured with practical margin, rather than interpreting numerical tolerances as operational reserve.

The default 12-hour horizon, customer/station candidate bounds, one-charge-per-gap restriction and full-power reservation can exclude physically feasible journeys. `INFEASIBLE_MODEL` therefore does not mean no solution exists under every possible route, charger chaining, horizon or power-sharing policy. Physical assistance is explicitly identified for health/stranding cases, not implied by every solver time limit. Geometry uses straight-line distance and fixed speed unless an explicit road matrix is supplied; production road routing is outside this simulator's model.

Fleet decomposition is a deliberate bounded-runtime choice. The solver allocates the most constrained vehicle first using reachable charger count, then earliest outstanding deadline, energy and VIN. Provisional reservations feed subsequent solves. This passes a two-vehicle fixture where one can reach only one charger and the other can use a second. It does not solve the joint fleet objective or guarantee global fleet feasibility. Each approved alternative is rechecked against current bookings, so selecting a different individual alternative can invalidate another proposed journey and requires replanning it.
